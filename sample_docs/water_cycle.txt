The Water Cycle

The water cycle, also known as the hydrologic cycle, describes the continuous
movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. Water changes
state between liquid, vapour, and ice as it moves through the cycle, but the
total amount of water on the planet stays roughly constant.

Evaporation is the process by which the sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and
rivers, turning it into water vapour that rises into the atmosphere. Plants also
release water vapour through a process called transpiration.

As water vapour rises and cools, it condenses into tiny droplets that form
clouds. This stage is called condensation. When the droplets in clouds grow
large and heavy enough, they fall back to the surface as precipitation in the
form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

Once precipitation reaches the ground, some of it flows over the surface as
runoff into rivers and oceans, while some soaks into the soil as infiltration,
replenishing groundwater. From there the water eventually returns to the oceans
and the cycle begins again.
